Karst Mountains to Canton

Eleven days across two provinces, entirely by train. Five nights in the karst country of Guangxi, two in Guilin, three at the best table in China. No flights, no airports, and the weather gets better as you go.

The shape

Two provinces. Zero flights.

Guangxi first for the landscape, Guangdong second for the food, and the high-speed railway does all the moving. Nobody sets foot in an airport between the wedding and the flight home. Three bases in eleven days, which leaves five nights in the first bed.

Why this shape

What the no-flights rule bought you

Dropping air travel took Xi'an and Chongqing off the table, and both of those were genuinely tempting. Here is what you got in exchange, because it is more than it sounds.

What you gained

  • Five nights in one bed. The single biggest comfort win available. No packing, no checkout, no morning where the bags go in the corridor.
  • No airport days. Two rail legs of under three hours, both starting late morning, both with the luggage handled and the seats together.
  • The warm half last. The trip ends seven degrees warmer than it starts. That is the right way round after two weeks of travel.
  • Direct flights home. Guangzhou flies non-stop to Amsterdam and several US hubs. Xi'an and Chongqing would both have meant a domestic connection first.

What you gave up, honestly

  • The Terracotta Army. The real cost of this decision. It is the one thing a first-time visitor is most likely to wish they had seen.
  • A second kind of food. Guangxi and Guangdong cooking are cousins. Xi'an wheat country would have been a sharper contrast.
  • Deep history. Nothing on this itinerary is older than the Ming, apart from the landscape itself, which is older than everything.
  • A second full day in Guangzhou. Three nights gives two full days, so Foshan and Shenzhen compete for the same slot. See the questions at the end.

Also left out

  • Longji rice terraces. Brown rather than green in March, two hours out of Guilin, and a long walk on slopes. Famous, and wrong for this month and this group.
  • Kaiping. The UNESCO watchtowers are genuinely interesting, and the overseas Chinese story connects to your American friends. But it is a full day of driving.
  • Hong Kong. Close, easy, and a different story than the one this trip tells.
  • Beijing. Worth its own trip, not three rushed days bolted on the end.

Still on the table

  • Foshan instead of Shenzhen on day 10. Ceramics, a kiln that has fired for five hundred years, and the hall Wing Chun came from. One hour out.
  • Chaozhou as a tail for anyone who wants it. Teochew food, gongfu tea, and Lina's family. Three hours by rail, and the parents are going anyway.
  • The full Guilin cruise instead of the short Xingping stretch, if the group would rather have four hours on the water than ninety minutes.
  • Kaiping as a long day out of Guangzhou, if the watchtowers matter more than a slow day does.
Ground conditions

Weather, and what the days will feel like

One honest note on the weather, since it drove the decision. Guangdong in late March is the good bit. Guangxi is cooler and mistier, and the mist is exactly what makes the karst look like the paintings, but it is not the sunny half.

Base Dates Late March °F Conditions Pack
Yangshuo 22 to 27 Mar 15 to 23 °C 59 to 73 Misty, rain likely, atmospheric Light layers, grippy shoes, umbrella
Guilin 27 to 29 Mar 15 to 22 °C 59 to 72 Overcast, mild, damp evenings The same, plus a light jacket at night
Guangzhou 29 Mar to 1 Apr 19 to 26 °C 66 to 79 Humid, warm, short showers Shirt sleeves, umbrella

One date to stay clear of. Qingming, the tomb-sweeping holiday, falls on 5 April 2027 and the entire country travels that weekend. This routing departs on 1 April, which is clear. Extending past 2 April means booked-out trains and packed sites.

And one on rail tickets. Chinese rail tickets go on sale 14 days ahead, and first class on popular routes can vanish the same morning. Both long legs need booking the hour the window opens, not the week after.

Mobility

  • Nothing on the fixed itinerary needs a climb. Everything that does is marked optional and has a flat alternative.
  • Yangshuo is done by electric cart and boat, not on foot.
  • Guilin is flat: a riverside walk, a walled palace compound, and an evening boat.
  • Guangzhou is flat with a metro that reaches everything, and Shamian is shaded.
  • The three things with real steps, Moon Hill, the Reed Flute Cave and the river banks at Xingping, are all optional.

Comfort standard

  • Four and five star hotels, four rooms. Countryside in Yangshuo, by the lakes in Guilin, central in Guangzhou.
  • Private minibus sized well above eight seats, same driver for each block.
  • First class rail on both long legs.
  • Luggage moved hotel to hotel and station to station. Nobody carries a bag up a step.
  • Local restaurants throughout. No hotel buffets, no set menus built for coach groups.

Pace, as promised

  • Nothing starts before 09:30. No exceptions on this version, because there is no site with a coach wave to beat.
  • One temple, palace or museum per day, maximum. Never three.
  • Two idle half-days and one completely empty day, with nothing booked at all.
  • Lunches are ninety minutes and sat down.
  • Both long transfers leave late morning, so no early starts on moving days either.

Where the food is the event

  • Yangshuo. Beer fish, cooked in the river it came out of, and a farmhouse cooking class.
  • Guilin. Rice noodles for breakfast, which is what the city actually eats.
  • Guangzhou. Dim sum from the carts, roast goose, and a seafood dinner ordered from the tank.
  • Nobody eats a Western breakfast for eleven days unless they want to.
Who travels with them

One host on the road, a licensed guide in each place

The host stays with the group the whole way and handles language, food, trains, luggage and anything that goes wrong. The history and the sightseeing come from a licensed local guide hired by the day. That split is what makes it possible to staff this with friends rather than an agency, and it is what keeps the cost near the target.

Before this becomes a booking

Five things we need from you

Shenzhen or Foshan on day 10?

Three nights in Guangzhou gives two full days, and these two want the same one. Shenzhen answers the technology and modern-China request. Foshan is ceramics, a five-hundred-year-old kiln and the home of Wing Chun. We would pick Shenzhen, but it is genuinely close.

Six travellers or eight?

The minibus, the hosts and the local guides cost the same either way, so the per-person figure moves by roughly USD 25 a day between six and eight. Eight is materially better value, and the vehicle is already sized for it.

Confirm 22 March to 1 April, flying home on the 1st.

Anything running past 2 April collides with Qingming. If people want longer, the right answer is a fourth night in Guangzhou, which also settles the Shenzhen and Foshan question above.

Which days do you want to keep for family and local contacts?

Day 4 in Yangshuo is completely empty, and the afternoons of days 5, 8 and 9 are free. Name the ones you want and everything else gets built around them.

Any mobility limits we have not been told about?

Knees, hips, stairs, walking distance, anything at all. It changes which items are marked optional, and it is far easier to plan around now than on the morning of.
